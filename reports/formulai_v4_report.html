Model report · 4 October 2026 · data through 2026 R15, Azerbaijan

FormulAI podium model v4

The data now runs through Baku, eight bugs that were quietly degrading predictions are fixed, and every candidate model was re-tested the way it is used: each race predicted only from the races before it. The new model's podium probabilities are 24% more accurate than the current production model's, and its podium picks match the best simple baseline. No approach, tree, linear or neural, reliably beats "take the top three on the grid" on podium picks alone.

Better probabilities, not more podiums

Compared race by race against the grid baseline, every model's podium-pick difference straddles zero: about one pick in thirty, inside the noise of 107 races. On log-loss the gradient-boosted models and the selected blend are clearly better than the grid, while the current production model is clearly worse. A podium hit rate near 2 in 3 is the sport's ceiling for this information, not a limitation of any one algorithm.

Machine learning or deep learning?

Machine learning, specifically gradient-boosted trees with a probabilistic ranking layer. In the lab the neural ListMLE ranker (an MLP trained on the Plackett-Luce likelihood, three seeds averaged) matched the grid baseline on picks and was worse calibrated than every tree model. Adding it to the blend did not help.

That is what the literature predicts at this scale. The data is 5,435 driver-race rows across 267 races, and the dominant signal (current car pace) shifts whenever the regulations reset.

  1. Grinsztajn, Oyallon & Varoquaux (2022), NeurIPS Datasets & Benchmarks. Tree-based models remain state of the art on medium-sized (~10k row) tabular data.
  2. Shwartz-Ziv & Armon (2022), Information Fusion 81. XGBoost generally beat deep tabular models outside the datasets they were designed on.
  3. McElfresh et al. (2023), NeurIPS Datasets & Benchmarks (176 datasets). The neural vs boosted-tree gap is often negligible; tuning GBDTs matters more than the model family.
  4. Erickson et al. (2025), TabArena, NeurIPS Datasets & Benchmarks. Deep tabular models catch up with GBDTs only with large tuning budgets and ensembling. Pretrained foundation models (Hollmann et al. 2025, TabPFN, Nature 637) lead on small datasets.
  5. van Kesteren & Bergkamp (2023), JQAS 19(4). A Bayesian rank-ordered logit on 2014–2021 attributes about 88% of driver/constructor variance to the car; Bell et al. (2016, JQAS 12) found 86% for 1979–2014.
  6. Burges (2010), LambdaMART (MSR-TR-2010-82); Xia et al. (2008), ListMLE, ICML. These are the ranking objectives used here.
  7. Peng et al. (2021), IPDPS, and Heilmeier et al. (2020), Applied Sciences 10. Sequence networks pay off on lap-by-lap timing data, where there are 20–100× more rows.

Where deep learning would earn its place: (1) a live in-race model over lap-by-lap timing, and (2) TabPFN as a pretrained, pointwise P(podium) member of the blend. TabPFN wasn't tested here because it needs a weights download.

What was wrong

Lapped cars counted as DNFs

Jolpica's 2023+ data says "Lapped" where older seasons said "+1 Lap". The code only recognised "Finished" and "+…", so hundreds of classified finishers became retirements. That corrupted the DNF head's labels and every reliability and form feature built on status.

Forecasts ran on an empty grid

The next-race prediction inserted placeholder results (every driver "P10, Pending") with no grid. The model, trained on real grids, saw grid=20 for the entire field. Forecasts now use a separate pre-qualifying model until qualifying runs, and nothing is written to the database.

Probabilities were near-uniform

A softmax over min-max-scaled scores at temperature 3 gave the favourite about 5.9%, and the field summed to 100% instead of 300%. These are now exact Plackett-Luce P(podium), calibrated out of sample.

Circuit features saw the future

Circuit DNF rate, quali spread and pit-stop history included the race being predicted and every later race. Circuit-family maps also used Jolpica IDs (marina_bay) where the database stores singapore, so several tracks fell through to the default.

The ranker mostly learned winners

F1-points relevance labels (25, 18, 15…) with LightGBM/XGBoost's default exponential gain make a win worth ~128× a second place. The new rankers use linear-gain top-10 labels.

Automation had been failing since August

Every scheduled run crashed on an argument-parsing error after ingestion, and GitHub has since disabled the workflow for inactivity. The committed Jolpica client fetched only ~5 races per season, and the automated pipeline "forecast" the last race already run.

The baseline gate was in-sample

The CI check scored the saved model on a season it had trained on. It now compares the season's walk-forward picks with the grid and quali baselines on the same races.

Stale and missing data

The database stopped at Zandvoort (R12). Madrid and Sepang had no circuit metadata, weather covered only 2022 onward, and sprint results weren't ingested. All are now filled. Sprint position was tested as a feature and added nothing measurable, so it isn't used yet.

What changed in the pipeline

Features

features/fast_features.py builds all 267 races in about 2 seconds, against roughly 30 minutes for the old builder. It adds in-season team pace, recent qualifying form, field-relative ranks and regulation-reset awareness. Every rolling value is joined strictly from earlier races, and a test proves it.

Model

models_v2/podium_predictor.py has separate post- and pre-qualifying blends (members in config/training_config.yaml) and an exact Plackett-Luce probability layer. The API prefers it and falls back to the legacy LTR.

Evaluation

scripts/model_lab.py runs the walk-forward comparison with paired bootstrap intervals and calibration tables. scripts/rolling_backtest.py publishes the season's out-of-sample picks plus the next-race forecast to both data/ and the frontend.

Automation

Incremental ingestion, a weather and sprint top-up, a cache that can't restore truncated seasons, deterministic outputs (CI only commits when the data changed), and an out-of-sample baseline gate.

Next steps

  1. Re-enable the GitHub workflow (it was disabled for inactivity). The first run does a full re-ingest of about 10 minutes, then incremental runs.
  2. Give the Hugging Face backend a database snapshot. data/db is git-ignored, so the hosted API has no history to build v4 features from and will fall back to the legacy model.
  3. Practice long-run pace (FP2) is the most promising new signal for pre-qualifying forecasts. The fp2_long_runs table exists but is empty.
  4. Try TabPFN as a pointwise blend member, and build a lap-by-lap sequence model for live races.
